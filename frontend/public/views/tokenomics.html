02 / Utility

What $ROBOTMONEY Does

One mechanism runs today: swap fees fund buybacks, and the tokens bought are burned. The two governance mechanisms below are designed, not built. Each card says which it is.

Allocation Authority (planned)

Not live. There is no ballot, no quorum and no vote contract. Allocation changes are proposed by the swarm and executed by the admin multisig. Moving that authority to holders is planned, with no ship date.

Inclusion Demand (planned)

Not live, and downstream of the vote above. The design: a token clears a published eligibility filter, then holders weight it, so ecosystems and agent treasuries would hold $ROBOTMONEY to push for inclusion. No filter and no ballot exist yet, so neither does the demand.

Supply Reduction

Live. The protocol's only revenue is its share of the 1% fee on every $ROBOTMONEY swap. There is no management fee. When that revenue covers a purchase, the protocol buys $ROBOTMONEY on the open market and burns it. The buyback record is below.

02b / Principles

Design Principles

The protocol is structured around four principles.

Fair launch

No team allocation. No pre-sale. No insider tranche. All tokens entered the Uniswap v4 pool through Clanker's liquidity staircase at launch.

Permanent liquidity

LP locked until 2100 via Clanker's locker contract. The protocol earns swap fees from its own pool rather than paying inflationary rewards to mercenary LPs.

Open vault

Vault logic is open and verifiable on Base. Allocation changes are proposed by the swarm and executed by the admin multisig. Handing that authority to token holders is planned, not built.

Bounded governance

When holder governance ships, it covers sleeve weights and nothing else. No vote on the protocol wallets, no marketing vote, no operations vote. The constraint is written down now, before there is anything to vote on.

03 / Governance

The swarm proposes. The multisig executes.

Two parties move the allocation today: an independent swarm that files signed takes, and the admin multisig that decides whether to apply the result. Token holders are not in that loop yet.

Daily Swarm Review

The swarm runs on a daily cadence and reviews one portfolio per session. The Robot Money allocation is one of the portfolios it reviews. Members file signed takes independently and cannot read each other's until the window closes. The recommendation vector is the arithmetic mean over the take set, so anyone holding the published takes can recompute it and check the number. Applying it to the vault is gated by the admin multisig. Sessions and takes are at /swarm.

Standing Weights

Nothing moves on its own. The current weights stand until the multisig applies a new proposal, so the fund always has a stated allocation. There is no quorum rule here, because there is no vote.

Four Sleeves

The allocation is four sleeves, not three buckets: Conservative DeFi Yield, Agent Tokens, Protocol Tokens, Real World Assets. Live weights and the date they were set are published at /allocation. No monthly holder vote sets them. Tokenholder voting on sleeve weights is planned.

The Inclusion Economy (planned)

None of this is built. It is the intended shape of tokenholder governance, written down so it can be checked against what ships. Two parts.

01

The filter would set the eligible set.

A token would have to clear a published quantitative filter before any holder could weight it, and holders could not add one that fails. No such filter runs today.

02

The vote would set the weighting within that set.

Ecosystems, agent treasuries and holders that wanted a qualifying token weighted would hold $ROBOTMONEY and vote on it. That is where structural demand for the token would come from. There is no vote contract, so today it does not.

The filter would govern eligibility. Holders would govern weighting. Both would be public. Neither exists yet.

04 / Buybacks

Discretionary Buybacks

Buybacks are funded by one thing: the protocol's share of the 1% fee on every $ROBOTMONEY swap. There is no management fee, and the 0.25% vault exit fee stays in the vault with the remaining depositors, so it is not protocol revenue either. That income is thin at this size, which is why buybacks are occasional and discretionary rather than scheduled. Purchased tokens are sent to the dead address (0x000…dEaD) and removed from circulation permanently.

06 / Precedents

Mechanism Precedents

Two established protocols inform the design.

C

Curve

Gauge-style allocation voting. Token holders direct where capital flows; projects acquire and hold the governance token to influence inclusion. Robot Money's planned governance borrows the pattern, and would gate the eligible set by an independent quantitative filter rather than by emissions, so influence could not compound through lock-up schemes that reward incumbents. The gauge vote itself is not built.

B

Botto

Minimal governance surface. Holders vote on allocation only, not on treasury, marketing or operations, and the agent executes a default if quorum is not met. Robot Money carries the same constraint into its planned governance, and the vault is open to third-party deposits, so the capital base is not bounded by the token's market cap.
